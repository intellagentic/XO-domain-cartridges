## 2. PRACTICE SECTIONS — Extracted from Real Business Outcomes

### DOMAIN: Commercial Lease Negotiation
**Practice 1: Lease Structure Flexibility in Distressed Scenarios**
- Analyze macro-economic + micro-tenant drivers; position tenant as "problem solver" to landlord
- Problem: Landlord resisting lease modifications during tenant distress
- Solution: Reframe as "keeping building occupied > vacant"; offer maintenance/cosmetic improvements in exchange for modification; tie to broader portfolio goal
- Source: Deep Valley settlement (2023), B2B DTLA lease re-negotiation (June 2023)

**Practice 2: Conditional Lease Language for Operator Transition**
- Lock operator flexibility into lease via "approved substitution" language
- Problem: Lease tied to original operating entity; landlord resists transfer if operator changes
- Solution: Draft substitution clause with operator criteria (insurance, financials, operating standards) rather than "named operator"; use covenant language for ongoing performance standards
- Anti-pattern: Static, operator-specific leases that become illiquid
- Source: Cheebo location transfers across Byte to Bite / Broadside / Kinetic entities

**Practice 3: NNN vs. Modified Gross Positioning in Multi-Location Portfolios**
- Match lease structure to cash flow predictability of tenant
- Problem: Multi-location operator (Cheebo) needs caps on variable expenses
- Solution: CAM caps tied to tenant revenue threshold; R&M caps with shared SG&A methodology; built-in adjustment mechanisms if tenant changes
- Example: Cheebo locations negotiated with variable-expense caps tied to revenue tiers; when revenue dropped, operator could access SG&A adjustment framework
- Source: HRPG deep valley, Crenshaw, and Burbank properties with Cheebo (2023–2024)

### DOMAIN: Distressed Asset Acquisition
**Practice 4: Sub-2% Operator Margin as Signal of Distress**
- If operating margin < 2%, transaction (lease modification, sale, or asset disposition) is imminent
- Example: Cheebo operations, when operating margins compressed to sub-2% in several locations, signaled capital raise necessity or asset sale trigger
- Applied in: Kinetic Eats acquisition by Integral Technology Group (Dec 2025–Jan 2026)
- Source: Monthly P&Ls across Cheebo locations (2023–2024), ITG APA analysis (Dec 2025)

**Practice 5: FF&E as Negotiation Leverage in Surrender Scenarios**
- In lease surrender/early termination, FF&E (fixtures, fittings, equipment) creates negotiating room
- Problem: Landlord demanding full liability for tenant improvements; tenant insolvent
- Solution: Offer FF&E (espresso machine, kitchen equipment, POS hardware) in exchange for tenant improvement write-down or early termination flexibility
- Example: B2B DTLA location — offered commercial equipment as part of surrender package vs. fighting $200K+ renovation liability
- Source: DTLA lease disputes (2024), kitchen liquidation coordination (2025)

**Practice 6: Distressed Tenant Notification & Settlement Pattern**
- Multi-step communication: demand → 30-day respond → offer settlement → escrow holding
- Applied: Multiple Cheebo locations (Burbank rent delinquency to Losvet, etc.)
- Key: Maintain landlord relationship (small portfolio, repeat partner) while managing payment plans and cleanup
- Source: Losvet/Burbank property management correspondence (2023–2024)
