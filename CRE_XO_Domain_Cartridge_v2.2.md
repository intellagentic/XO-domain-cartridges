## 2. CRE PRACTICE SECTIONS

### DOMAIN 1: Commercial Lease Negotiation

**CRE Practice 1: Lease Structure Flexibility in Distressed Scenarios**
- Analyze macro-economic + micro-tenant drivers; position tenant as "problem solver" to landlord
- Problem: Landlord resisting lease modifications during tenant distress
- Solution: Reframe as "keeping building occupied > vacant"; offer maintenance/cosmetic improvements in exchange for modification; tie to broader portfolio goal
- Source: Property Iota settlement (2023), B2B DTLA lease re-negotiation (June 2023)

**CRE Practice 2: Conditional Lease Language for Operator Transition**
- Lock operator flexibility into lease via "approved substitution" language
- Problem: Lease tied to original operating entity; landlord resists transfer if operator changes
- Solution: Draft substitution clause with operator criteria (insurance, financials, operating standards) rather than "named operator"; use covenant language for ongoing performance standards
- Anti-pattern: Static, operator-specific leases that become illiquid
- Source: Food-Tech Entity location transfers across Food-Tech entities / Multi-Brand entities

**CRE Practice 3: NNN vs. Modified Gross Positioning in Multi-Location Portfolios**
- Match lease structure to cash flow predictability of tenant
- Problem: Multi-location operator needs caps on variable expenses
- Solution: CAM caps tied to tenant revenue threshold; R&M caps with shared SG&A methodology; built-in adjustment mechanisms if tenant changes
- Source: FIG Property Iota, Property Kappa, and [Valley Location] properties with Food-Tech Entity (2023–2024)

### DOMAIN 2: Distressed Asset Acquisition & Disposition

**CRE Practice 4: Sub-2% Operator Margin as Signal of Distress**
- If operating margin < 2%, transaction (lease modification, sale, or asset disposition) is imminent
- Applied: Food-Tech Entity operations margin compression → Multi-Brand Food Operator APA to Acquiring Entity (Dec 2025–Jan 2026)
- Source: Monthly P&Ls across Food-Tech Entity locations (2023–2024), Acquiring Entity APA analysis

**CRE Practice 5: FF&E as Negotiation Leverage in Surrender Scenarios**
- In lease surrender/early termination, FF&E (fixtures, fittings, equipment) creates negotiating room
- Problem: Landlord demanding full liability for tenant improvements; tenant insolvent
- Solution: Offer FF&E (espresso machine, kitchen equipment, POS hardware) in exchange for TI write-down or early termination flexibility
- Source: DTLA lease disputes (2024), kitchen liquidation coordination (2025)

**CRE Practice 6: Distressed Tenant Notification & Settlement Pattern**
- Multi-step communication: demand → 30-day respond → offer settlement → escrow holding
- Key: Maintain landlord relationship (small portfolio, repeat partner) while managing payment plans and cleanup
- Source: Property Management Co A / [Valley Location] property management correspondence (2023–2024)

**CRE Practice 7: Settlement Agreement as Relationship Preservation Tool**
- In distressed lease scenarios, offer: structured payment plan + minor tenant improvement (cleanup, cosmetic fixes) + release of all claims
- Both parties get certainty; relationship preserved for future dealings
- Source: Property Iota settlement; Property Management Co A / [Valley Location] payment arrangements

**CRE Practice 8: Lease Amendment for Mid-Term Modifications**

### DOMAIN 9: Lender Underwriting & Loan Optimization

**CRE Practice 39: Management Fee vs. Admin Fee Distinction in NNN Leases**
- In lender underwriting, management fee and admin fee are often conflated. Many leases include BOTH: a 10% admin fee on CAM/tax/insurance AND a separate management fee recovery.
- When lender underwrites only admin fee, the missing management recovery = direct cashflow upside
- Applied to 7 Property Zeta tenants: El Pollo Loco ($3,409), For Your Nails ($1,273), Ramona Donuts ($1,270), URI Travel ($1,193), Pizza Hut ($2,107), Yoshinoya ($2,885), Subway ($1,318) = $13,435/yr total = ~$100K loan proceeds uplift
- Source: Lender Analyst BG/CMBS Conduit analysis, Property Zeta (Aug 2014)

**CRE Practice 40: Prop 13 Protection Expiration Analysis**
- California Prop 13 protections in commercial leases often have time limits (e.g., initial term + first option only). When a tenant is in a later option period, the protection expires.
- Lender underwriters frequently miss this, underwriting with Prop 13 protection when it has expired
- Applied: Sherwin Williams at Property Delta — Prop 13 protection expired in 2nd option period. 8.4% pro-rata = $7,676/yr uplift. Also Radio Shack — protection didn't apply to 1st or 2nd sale after lease start, but cap ($3.84 PSF) still exceeded current ($2.70 PSF)
- Source: Lender Analyst BG analysis, Property Delta (Aug 2014)

**CRE Practice 41: Hidden Reimbursement Covenants in NNN Lease Language**
- Some NNN leases include covenants where tenant reimburses a specified % of taxes + insurance even though base lease shows "NNN"

- Applied: AutoZone at Property Delta — reimbursement covenant at 13.8% = $12,664 uplift; Seafood City with 5% YoY expense cap ($3,402 base May 1997, compounded 17 years = $7,797.44/mo cap) revealed in fine print

### DOMAIN 16: Restaurant Location Development & Entitlements

**CRE Practice 62: 15-Permit Pipeline Management for Restaurant Openings**
- Standard California restaurant opening requires 15 permits: health (county), liquor (state ABC), fire (local FD), building (city), electrical, plumbing, mechanical, hood/ventilation, curb cut, signage, parking, ADA, DAS (deaf access), OSE (online submission), and conditional use permit (CUP) if not-by-right
- Timeline: 90-180 days critical path (health + ABC + fire in parallel); sequential delays (DAS after health approval) extend to 6+ months
- Cost: $15K-$30K in permits + engineer/attorney fees
- Source: Airtable "B2B | RE: Culinary Centers" (148-field location database), Food-Tech Entity site openings (2020–2025)

**CRE Practice 63: ABC License Approval Factors & Timeline**
- ABC (Alcoholic Beverage Control) approval: 40-60 day standard + public notice objection period (30-45 days additional if objections filed)
- Factors: neighborhood objection risk, distance to schools/parks (300-600 ft zones), local control ordinances (LCOs)
- Risk mitigation: community outreach (neighborhood meetings), CUP bundling (get conditional use permit approval first), social benefits narrative (jobs, local sourcing)
- Applied: all 15 Food-Tech Entity restaurant locations required ABC licenses; highest-risk: [Location 9 - Hollywood], [Location 2 - Mid LA] (school proximity)
- Source: ABC process documentation (2020–2024), [Location 9], [Location 2] approval timelines

**CRE Practice 64: Health Department Site Assessment — 17-Category Inspection Checklist**
- Pre-opening health inspection covers: ventilation adequacy (CFM capacity for menu type), water/sewer (capacity + separate grease trap), food storage (temperature zones, contamination separation), prep surfaces (NSF approved materials), handwashing stations (placement + hot water), utility connections (natural gas pressures), equipment spacing (3-foot clearance minimum), lighting (50+ FC kitchen, 20 FC front), flooring (sealed, slip-resistant)
- Tool: 17-category site assessment checklist (Airtable database), scored pass/fail per category
- Pre-approval process: submit floor plan → health dept preliminary review → operator remediation → health inspection → pass
- Source: Airtable checklist (17 categories, 148-field database), Food-Tech Entity site assessment database (2020–2025)

**CRE Practice 65: CUP (Conditional Use Permit) Strategy & Opposition Management**
- CUP required if restaurant use not-by-right in zoning (most LA zones allow QSR retail; some require CUP for full-service + alcohol)
- Timeline: 45-90 days (application → planning dept review → planning commission hearing → possible appeal)
- Opposition risk: neighborhood residents, competing restaurants, community groups
- Mitigation: early community engagement, local hire commitments, operating hour restrictions, noise/odor mitigation measures
- Applied: Food-Tech Entity CUP applications for [Location 9 - Hollywood] (entertainment overlay district) + [Location 14 - Koreatown] (residential adjacency)
- Source: CUP approval documents (2021–2023), planning commission hearing notes

**CRE Practice 66: Liquor License Transfer & Tie-Down Compliance**
- ABC license transfer: new operator assumes ongoing obligations (no changes to hours, location, ownership > 20% without ABC approval)
- "Tie-down" = ABC conditional approval limiting operational parameters (e.g., "beer/wine only, no spirits"; "closed by 11pm")
- Strategic issue: inherited tie-downs may restrict multi-brand usage (e.g., Food-Tech Entity couldn't serve spirits in inherited license from prior operator)
- Workaround: surrender old license + new application (resets timeline to 60-90 days but clears tie-downs)
- Applied: Food-Tech Entity locations (#6, #7, #12, #13, #14) inheritance tie-down issues → remediation via surrender strategy (2023–2024)
- Source: ABC license transfer documents (2020–2024), Food-Tech Entity tie-down analysis

**CRE Practice 67: Demographic Site Selection — Ordering Propensity Metrics**

**CRE Practice 68: Lease Escalation Timing for Multi-Year Build Commitment**
- Restaurant build: 3-month pre-opening build-out (FF&E installation, final inspections) + 2-4 month proof-of-concept period
- Lease strategy: 0% rent during build (3 months) + 50% rent during POC (2-4 months) + ramp to full rent over 12-24 months
- Landlord protection: personal guarantee + tie-down to operator (operator default = license revocation + immediate surrender)

- Strategic: preserves operator's ability to keep best-performing locations under one entity, transfer weak performers to different entity = negotiating flexibility with landlord
